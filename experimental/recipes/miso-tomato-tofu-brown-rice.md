# Miso-Tomato Braised Tofu over Brown Rice

**Status:** Not yet tried
**Inspiration:** Flavor-matrix exploration — tomato + miso share methional (savory cooked-potato compound) and the resulting umami stack reverse-engineers the tomato-tofu dish Emma loved at a Chinese restaurant, without leaning on heavy soy/oyster sauce sodium.

## Quick stats

- **Active time:** ~20 min (plus ~45 min rice, mostly hands-off)
- **Total time:** ~50 min if starting rice from scratch; ~25 min if rice is already cooked
- **Dishes:** 1 pan + 1 rice pot
- **Servings:** 2
- **Protein:** ~25g per serving (firm tofu) or ~20g (silken)
- **Sodium:** moderate — ~500–600 mg per serving with white miso + low-sodium soy. Controllable.

## Ingredients

- 1 block firm tofu, pressed and cubed (or silken for the original Chinese homestyle softness — more delicate, more authentic)
- 2 medium tomatoes, cut into wedges (or 1 cup cherry tomatoes, halved)
- 1 tbsp tomato paste (concentrated umami)
- 2 tbsp white miso (shiro miso — milder and less salty than red)
- 1 tbsp mirin (or 1 tsp sugar + a splash of rice vinegar)
- 1 tbsp low-sodium soy sauce
- 3 cloves garlic, minced
- 1/8 tsp white pepper (warmth without going gingery)
- 1 tbsp neutral oil + 1 tsp toasted sesame oil for finishing
- 2 scallions, sliced thin
- 1 cup cooked brown rice (~1/2 cup dry, cooked separately)
- *Optional:* small handful of frozen peas or broccoli florets for color and fiber
- *Optional:* pinch of red pepper flakes if you want heat

## Method

1. **Start the brown rice first** (~45 min). When the rice is ~20 min in, begin the dish.
2. **Sear the tofu.** Press tofu briefly with a clean towel, cube into 1-inch pieces. Heat 1 tbsp neutral oil in a non-stick or well-seasoned pan over medium-high. Sear tofu until golden on at least two sides, ~6–8 min total. Remove and set aside.
3. **Build the aromatics.** Same pan, lower heat to medium. Add the minced garlic and white pepper. Stir 30 seconds until fragrant.
4. **Add the tomatoes.** Add tomatoes and tomato paste. Let them break down, ~3–4 min, until the wedges soften and the paste deepens in color.
5. **Make the miso slurry.** In a small bowl, whisk the miso with mirin, low-sodium soy sauce, and 2–3 tbsp water until smooth. (Miso doesn't dissolve well in hot fat directly — pre-mixing prevents lumps.)
6. **Combine.** Pour the miso slurry into the pan. Simmer 2 min so the flavors meld and the sauce tightens slightly.
7. **Return the tofu.** Fold gently to coat — silken tofu wants very gentle hands here; firm tofu can take a sturdier stir. Simmer 2–3 min more so the tofu absorbs sauce.
8. **Finish.** Off heat: drizzle the toasted sesame oil and top with scallions.
9. **Serve** over brown rice. A glass of water with lemon alongside.

## Constraint notes (Emma-specific)

- **Tomato** is citrate-rich and low-oxalate — actively helpful for kidney stones.
- **Miso** brings the bulk of the sodium. 2 tbsp white miso = ~700 mg sodium total, ~350 mg per serving. Well within budget if the rest of the day is moderate.
- **No ginger** — per preferences, savory dishes lean better without it. White pepper fills the warmth gap and is more authentic to traditional Chinese tomato-tofu builds.
- **No spinach, no high-oxalate greens** — broccoli or peas if you want extra veg.
- **All ingredients low-purine.**

## Variations

- **Protein boost:** sprinkle 1 tbsp hemp seeds over the bowl (+10g protein), or stir a beaten egg into the rice halfway through cooking the sauce (egg-fried-rice style underneath the tofu).
- **Higher heat:** a generous pinch of red pepper flakes with the garlic, or a small spoon of doubanjiang with the miso (watch sodium).
- **Different greens:** wilt a handful of kale or bok choy into the sauce just before adding the tofu back.
- **Different grain:** quinoa or farro instead of brown rice. Quinoa adds ~6g protein per cup.
- **Fancier version:** finish with a few drops of black vinegar instead of (or alongside) sesame oil.
