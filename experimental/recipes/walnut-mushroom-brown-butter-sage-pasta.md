# Walnut-Mushroom Brown Butter Sage Pasta

**Status:** Not yet tried — planning to make on upcoming vacation with Alex
**Inspiration:** Flavor-matrix exploration — walnut and mushroom both carry 1-octen-3-ol (the earthy, raw-mushroom compound). Brown butter brings nutty Maillard depth that bridges them, sage adds herbaceous lift. Restaurant-level effect, kitchen-pantry effort.

## Quick stats

- **Active time:** ~25–30 min
- **Total time:** ~30 min
- **Dishes:** 1 pasta pot + 1 large skillet
- **Servings:** 2 (easy to double for 4 if you want leftovers)
- **Protein:** ~20g per serving (more with chickpea pasta, see variations)
- **Sodium:** moderate — controllable via parmesan amount and salting the pasta water judiciously
- **Vacation-friendly:** simple ingredient list (~7 items beyond pantry staples), low equipment burden, no special tools needed

## Ingredients

- 8 oz tagliatelle, pappardelle, or fettuccine (any wide flat pasta)
- 12 oz mushrooms — mix of cremini and shiitake if you can find them, or all cremini, sliced ~1/4" thick
- 1/2 cup walnut halves, roughly chopped
- 3 tbsp unsalted butter (must be real butter — margarine won't brown)
- 12 fresh sage leaves (one small bunch)
- 2 cloves garlic, minced
- 1/2 cup finely grated parmesan, plus more for finishing
- 1 lemon (you'll use the zest and a small squeeze)
- 1 tbsp neutral oil (for the mushrooms)
- Salt + freshly cracked black pepper
- *Optional:* pinch of red pepper flakes

## Method

1. **Pasta water first.** Bring a big pot of well-salted water to a boil. Cook the pasta 1 minute less than package directions for al dente. **Reserve ~1 cup of pasta water before draining.**

2. **Toast the walnuts.** While the pasta cooks: heat a large dry skillet over medium. Add the chopped walnuts, toast 2–3 min until fragrant and lightly browned. Remove to a plate.

3. **Sear the mushrooms.** Same skillet, turn to medium-high. Add 1 tbsp neutral oil and the sliced mushrooms in a single layer (don't crowd — work in two batches if needed). Don't move them for 2–3 min — let them get a real sear. Stir, cook another 3–4 min until deeply browned and shrunk by about a third. Add the minced garlic and a pinch of salt in the last 30 seconds. Move mushrooms to the walnut plate.

4. **Brown the butter.** Same skillet, medium heat. Add the 3 tbsp butter. Let it melt, then foam, then *watch the milk solids* — they'll turn from white to tan to golden-brown over ~3–4 minutes. Swirl the pan as it goes. When it smells nutty and looks deeply golden, drop in the sage leaves. They'll sizzle and crisp in 30–45 seconds. If you want crispy sage on top, fish them out with a slotted spoon onto a paper towel; otherwise just leave them in.

5. **Bring it all together.** Take the pan off heat. Add the cooked pasta directly to the butter pan along with a generous splash of pasta water (~1/3 cup to start). Toss vigorously — the starch + butter + water emulsifies into a silky sauce. Add the mushrooms, walnuts, and half the parmesan. Toss again. Add more pasta water if it tightens up too much — you want it glossy, not dry.

6. **Finish.** Season aggressively with cracked black pepper (and red pepper flakes if using). Add a small squeeze of lemon and the zest of about half the lemon — it brightens everything. Taste and adjust salt.

7. **Plate.** Mound into shallow bowls. Crumble the crispy sage leaves on top. Shower with more parmesan. Serve immediately.

## Constraint notes (Emma-specific)

- **Kidney stones:** Walnuts are moderate-oxalate, but 1/2 cup split between 2 servings is ~1/4 cup each — comfortably in "occasionally fine" territory. Cremini mushrooms are low-oxalate, low-purine. Sage, butter, parmesan all fine.
- **Sodium:** the main sources are the pasta water salt and the parmesan. Both controllable — use a moderate hand on each. Don't salt the dish further until you've tasted with the parmesan in.
- **Insulin-conscious:** the wide flat pasta will spike less than a small pasta because the surface-to-mass ratio is lower (more sauce per bite, less pure starch). Whole-wheat option below if you want it lower-glycemic.
- **All ingredients low-purine.**

## Variations

- **Higher protein:** swap to chickpea pasta (Banza or similar) — adds ~10–12g protein per serving. Toss with extra butter since chickpea pasta drinks sauce.
- **Whole-grain option:** whole-wheat tagliatelle works; a little nuttier and lower-glycemic.
- **Wine pairing for vacation:** an unoaked white (Pinot Grigio, Vermentino, dry Riesling) or a light red like Pinot Noir or Gamay. The brown butter likes the contrast.
- **Add greens:** a handful of arugula or baby kale folded in at the end with the parmesan adds color and a peppery bite. Spinach is the obvious choice but skip it (high oxalate).
- **Make it vegan:** swap butter for olive oil + a teaspoon of miso for the umami; use nutritional yeast instead of parmesan. The brown-butter magic is lost, but the dish still works.
- **Mushroom upgrade:** add 1/4 oz dried porcini, rehydrated in hot water for 20 min, chopped and added with the fresh mushrooms. Use the soaking liquid (strained!) in place of some pasta water for triple-mushroom depth.
- **Restaurant move:** finish with a tiny drizzle of truffle oil if you have it. Don't tell anyone how easy this was.
